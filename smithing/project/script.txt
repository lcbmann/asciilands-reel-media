I want players to be willing to cross a massive map just to buy a sword.
Not just because of what it’s made from, but because of who made it.
I’m building Asciilands around a player-run economy. Weapons and tools made by players, for players.
But for that to be interesting, people need a reason to buy from each other instead of just making everything themselves.
Rare materials are one reason. Better damage and durability make something worth paying for.
But if anyone with the same ingredients can click a button and make exactly the same sword, there’s not much reason to care who made it.
I wanted being good at making things to have value, too.
So in Asciilands, you shape the blade yourself. You control the heat, choose where to strike, and work the metal into shape.
Two players can start with the same steel and end up with very different swords. Better forging makes them hit harder and last longer.
And that shape you hammered out? That’s the actual sword you keep. You can give it a name, and your maker’s mark stays on it when someone else buys it.
The basic metals are forgiving. The higher-tier stuff takes more skill to work.
A good smith could build a shop, and a reputation. Someone asks who forged your masterwork sword, you point them towards that player, and suddenly they have another customer.
You’re not just paying for the materials and a bit of someone’s time. You’re paying for their skill.
But does this actually make crafting more rewarding, or just turn it into a chore? Tell me in the comments.
